Good Visualization:

Electricity generation per capita (2022) #VOTD
https://public.tableau.com/app/profile/rush1056/viz/Electricitygenerationpercapita2022/ElectricityGenerationpercapita2022

This visualization displays the electricity generation capacity for each world country and territory. Each country is grouped by region and furthermore classified by energy source type features.

Reasons for why it is a good visualization:

It manages cognitive load elegantly. There is a lot of information being digested for this visualization. We have electricity generation per capita in the year 2022, Per capita generation energy source type, Average generation per energy source from 1985-2022 and World trends from energy source from 1985-2022, all multiplied by 208 country and territory profiles.

What it does exceptionally, is the use of drop-down menus to switch from the different graphs and diagrams depending on the aspect you want to focus your attention, thus managing the Extraneousness of the subject in a dashboard kind of way.

Although the main Tree chart could be considered “Rare”, the visualization manages this figure’s potential cognitive complexity by allowing you to switch (via drop-down menu) to bar/line graphs per country/territory providing a Familiar data visualization option.

This visualization also handles Working Memory effectively. Energy source types of colors are standardized throughout the visualization for constant reference without the excess of labels. Furthermore, for each year between 1985 and 2022 that you can set a visual reference line per each country’s yearly electricity generation by source type’s chart so you can easily compare where each one stands in any given year within the timeframe.


Bad visualization:
Agricultural in The BRIC Countries | VOTD
https://public.tableau.com/app/profile/ratnesh2928/viz/AgriculturalinTheBRICNations/Aggriculture

This visualization attempts to display BRIC countries’ agricultural trends, sources and proportions in relation to their populations.

Reasons for why it is a bad visualization:

Starting off with Factual Basis, this visualization does not mention anywhere within the figures the source from where the information was taken from. This makes it hard to accept the information being shown as accurate or true and thus has low provenance rhetoric. Furthermore, certain charts such as the population diagram does not make a true comparison of each country’s population with a whole; one cannot define which whole is the population size of each country comparing itself to.

Focusing on cognitive load, this visualization comes off as very busy and dense where the digestion of the data can be difficult. Too many chunks of textual information, combined with a dense network of data, makes it difficult to read the figure clearly without having to rely too much on memory.

Lastly, the overall style of the visualization does not help readability any further. A strong/stark color palette distracts the viewer from the overall information. Overly forcing certain infographics into charts (such as the tractor wheel being a pie chart or the proportion of agriculture and GDP ad wheat grains) makes it difficult to read the data accurately.

Improvements for the “good visualization” analyzed above:

This visualization could be further improved by finding a more concise way of representing the sheer number of mini charts in the “Electricity generation by Country & by source” portion. Since the overall strategy here is of like a dashboard, one concise solution would be three heat maps (for each energy source) with the 208 tiles (for each country/territory) that darkens or lightens depending on the percentage of that energy source. We could also add a toggle bar to navigate throughout the analyzed timeframe of 1985-2022.

Lastly, the Tree diagram could be further improved to be even more engaging to the user by bringing up the trend data directly on each country’s branch. When resting we could see the overall trends (albeit with a different aspect ratio but standardized within every branch) and when a branch is selected, the data presents itself accurate in terms of numbers. This way we can visualize outlying electrical generation trends as well.

Improvements for the “bad visualization” analyzed above:

Beginning with citing sources in any blank corner of the visualization is crucial. If done properly it wouldn’t even be a hinderance towards the artistic intent of the visualization.

Another improvement could be the use of a more subtle color palette; even keeping the same palette but with lower tones/hues could greatly improve readability between texts and avoid color being a distractor from the actual information.

Lastly, the infographic/charts, albeit engaging, cannot come at the cost of data accuracy representation when the intention IS to represent that data accurately. For the population diagram, an easy fix would be to show the whole as the total combined population of BRIC countries and fill out each country’s part. For the % of land dedicated to agriculture, an easier fix would be to use tractor symbols to account for a percentual comparison between countries. Lastly, for the agriculture as % of GDP, an easier fix would be to make each grain to represent whole percentages (i.e. one grain = 5%) and scale down the number of “grains” needed to make a whole wheat stem (or 100%). This way one can truly appreciate the differences between each country’s GDP percentage.
